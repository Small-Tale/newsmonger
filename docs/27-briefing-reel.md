# 27 — The Briefing Reel

Everything the app produces today is read where it was made. The feed is a local page; the exports (Markdown, JSON, Atom) are text with links, aimed at whatever reader the user already lives in. None of it is *shown to anyone else*.

The briefing reel is the first artifact meant to leave. It renders the day's stories as a sequence of designed cards — one self-contained animated SVG, optionally narrated and optionally exported as video — using [domotion](https://github.com/brianwestphal/domotion) (`domotion-svg`), which turns real HTML/CSS into a portable animated SVG by capturing how Chromium paints it.

See also: [8 — Article Images](8-article-images.md) (where the photos come from and why they are proxied), [21 — Export and Feed](21-export-and-feed.md) (the existing text exports and the scope rules this reuses), [3 — UI](3-ui.md) (the brand tokens and the wordmark), [5 — Desktop App](5-desktop-app.md) (the packaging cost of a browser engine).

## Status: design decided (NEWS-172), nothing built

The feasibility investigation is complete (NEWS-165): the pipeline works, a 5-scene 1080×1920 reel renders in ~10.7 s, and the app's story fields map onto a story card with nothing invented. What follows is the specification the implementation tickets work against.

## 27.1 — What a reel is

- **FR-27.1** *(Design only)* A reel is an ordered sequence of **scenes**: a title card, one card per story, and a closing card. It renders to **one self-contained animated SVG** — no external fonts, images or scripts — so it embeds with a plain `<img>` and scales without artifacts.

- **FR-27.2** *(Design only)* Story selection reuses the export scope rules rather than inventing its own: newest-first, off-topic flagged stories excluded (FR-21.2), and the same `scope` parameter shape as the export endpoints (FR-21.1). A reel is a *view* of the same archive, and two different definitions of "the recent stories" would be a bug waiting to happen.

- **FR-27.3** *(Design only)* Formats follow domotion's presets, **vertical first**: `reel` 1080×1920 is the primary target and the only one the first implementation must support. `square` 1080×1080 and `landscape` 1920×1080 follow — the type scale adapts per format, so they are cheaper once the vertical design is settled, but they are not free and a tall story card does not become a wide one by rescaling.

## 27.2 — The attribution posture

Everything below is a **decision, not a default**. A shared card takes a publisher's copyrighted photograph and a model-written summary of their article, stamps this product's name on them, and publishes the result under the user's account. That is a materially different act from showing the same two things privately in a feed, and it was settled deliberately (NEWS-172) rather than allowed to fall out of an implementation.

- **FR-27.4** *(Decided, NEWS-172)* Story cards **carry the publisher's lead image**. It is what makes a card worth sharing, and a reel of text cards is a document rather than something anyone posts. The cost is accepted knowingly, and paid for by FR-27.5.

- **FR-27.5** *(Decided, NEWS-172)* **The outlet is a first-class element of every card, not a footnote.** `outletFor()` already resolves the model's stated outlet, falling back to the URL's registrable domain — the same value the feed shows. On a card it gets real typographic weight alongside the source count and date. If the artifact carries someone's photograph and a summary of their reporting, the reader must be able to see whose work it is without looking for it.

- **FR-27.6** *(Decided, NEWS-172)* **The AI disclosure lives on the closing card, not on every story card.** "Summaries generated by AI from the linked sources" appears once, at the end, rather than as a badge repeated on every card that would compete with the outlet name for the same attention.

  The known weakness: a *single* card screenshotted out of the reel carries no disclosure. That is accepted — the reel is the unit being shared, and a per-card badge is a real cost on every card to cover a case that is a screenshot of a screenshot. Revisit if single-card sharing becomes a first-class feature rather than something a user can do with any image.

- **FR-27.7** *(Decided, NEWS-172)* The reel **closes on a Newsmonger card** carrying the **wordmark** (FR-3.58 — the real asset, themed to the card's background per FR-3.58a), the story count and date, and the FR-27.6 disclosure line. It is both the sign-off and the only branding in the reel; story cards themselves stay unbranded so the outlet's name is the only attribution competing for attention on them.

## 27.3 — Images: the proxy is load-bearing

- **FR-27.8** *(Design only)* Capture pages must reference images **only** through the local cache — `http://127.0.0.1:<port>/api/image/<hash>`, or the cached file read directly from `<data-dir>/images/`. Domotion inlines whatever the page loads, so a page that referenced a publisher URL would put **Chromium** on the open internet, silently undoing FR-8.4's guarantee that the app makes zero third-party requests — and it would do so invisibly, since the rendered output looks identical either way.

  This is worth an assertion in the generator rather than a comment. The failure is silent, and the property it breaks is one the project verified deliberately.

- **FR-27.9** *(Design only)* Cached images are **downscaled to the slot's rendered size** before embedding. A self-contained SVG base64-inlines every photo at ~1.33× its byte size: the investigation's 5-scene reel was 611 KB, of which ~535 KB was three images and ~75 KB was every glyph and vector in all five scenes. Publisher artwork runs 1200–2400 px against a ~1080 px slot, so an un-downscaled 10-story reel plausibly lands at 10–20 MB — too large to share, which defeats the feature.

- **FR-27.10** *(Design only)* **A missing image is the normal case, not an edge case** (FR-8.2 — roughly a third of stories). The card design must have a deliberate no-image treatment that looks intentional, not a photo card with a hole in it.

## 27.4 — Timing and audio

- **FR-27.11** *(Design only)* Where a reel is narrated, **the audio is the clock**. Narration is synthesized first and measured, and each scene's duration is derived from its clip — not the reverse. Domotion takes explicit per-scene durations, so this is the natural direction; fitting speech to a pre-set animation is what forces drift correction.

- **FR-27.12** *(Design only)* In-app playback **slaves the animation to the audio** rather than synchronizing two independent clocks. The generated SVG is pure CSS `@keyframes` with no SMIL, all on one master period, so `document.getAnimations()` reaches every animation and one playhead drives the whole reel. Each frame, the playhead is set from `audio.currentTime` and the animations are seeked to it. There is no drift to correct because nothing runs free.

  This requires the SVG **inline in the document** — an `<img>`-embedded SVG is script-isolated and silent, which is the reason a shared reel file has no narration.

- **FR-27.13** *(Design only)* Every ambient loop's period must **divide the master timeline**. `svg-to-video` defaults its duration to one seamless loop, computed as the LCM of every animation period in the document: an investigation reel with a 16.4 s master timeline and one decorative 900 ms button pulse exported **147.6 s** of video — nine copies — because LCM(16.4, 0.9) = 147.6. Pass `--duration` explicitly regardless.

## 27.5 — What this costs

- **FR-27.14** *(Design only)* Rendering requires **Playwright's Chromium**, which enters the sidecar's dependency closure that `scripts/build-sidecar.sh` stages wholesale into the release bundle (FR-5.3). The dependency was accepted knowingly. Two things it commits us to, neither of which should be assumed to work:

  - The hardened-runtime entitlements were scoped to exactly **one** JS runtime (FR-5.6). A second browser engine spawning its own subprocesses needs the entitlement set re-verified, not merely extended — this is the failure mode that signs, notarizes and staples cleanly and then dies at launch on someone else's Mac.
  - Playwright normally fetches its browser on first use. A packaged app cannot rely on that at runtime, so Chromium must be staged into the bundle at build time and resolved from there.

- **FR-27.15** *(Design only)* Rendering is a **background job with progress**, never a synchronous request handler. A 5-scene reel measured 10.7 s and a real digest is longer; video export runs at roughly 2.4× realtime on top of that. The check runner and `src/client/discover-progress.ts` already establish the pattern.

- **FR-27.16** *(Design only)* Video export additionally needs **ffmpeg**, an external binary the user installs. Unlike domotion it cannot be bundled with the sidecar, so its absence must be detected and reported actionably rather than surfacing as a failed render.
